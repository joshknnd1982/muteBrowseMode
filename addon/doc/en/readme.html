Mute Browse Mode

NVDA loves to talk when a web page or an email opens. It reads out the name of the page, then the word "document", then the first line. Every single time.

This add-on tells it to be quiet, so you can start reading the page yourself with the arrow keys.

It also tidies up some noisy bits of Microsoft Outlook, and it lets you use NVDA's own find box with control+F.

Where the settings are

Press NVDA+n for the NVDA menu, then Preferences, then Settings, then Browse Mode. Everything this add-on adds is on that page, underneath NVDA's own settings.

The first setting starts on Normal, so NVDA carries on as usual until you change it. The page summary is the one thing that is switched on from the start.

Setting 1: Mute browse mode

This is the main one. It has three choices.

Silence all browsing

NVDA says nothing when a page or an email opens. No page name, no first line, and no "Loading document". You read it yourself, and reading works exactly as it always did.

Play tones

The same quiet, but you hear three quick beeps, going up, once the page is ready. Now you know when to start reading.

Normal

NVDA behaves as if you had never installed the add-on. This is how it starts out.

Setting 2: Announce a page summary when a page has loaded

When a web page finishes loading, this tells you how big it is instead of reading out its name. It also has three choices.

Speak the summary

NVDA says:

Loading complete
Page has 8 regions, 57 headings and 196 links

Regions are the big areas of a page, like the menu across the top or the main story in the middle. This is the choice you start with.

Play tones

Three quick beeps instead of the words. If the first setting is on beeps too, you still only hear one lot.

Normal

No summary at all.

When you do not get a summary

The summary is for web browsers only. Outlook never gets one, because Outlook is the place this add-on is meant to be quiet.

You also will not get one if:

  • the page loaded in a tab you are not looking at;
  • you pressed a key while it was loading, which means you have stopped waiting and started using it;
  • you have NVDA set to read the whole page out loud by itself. The add-on never talks over that.

Check box: Say "loading complete" before the page summary

Ticked to start with. Untick it and the summary is just "Page has 8 regions, 57 headings and 196 links", with nothing in front of it.

Check box: Links are on their own line

Not ticked to start with. Tick it and every link and button in an Outlook email gets a line of its own, so you can press the down arrow to go from one to the next.

Without it, a line that reads "comment this is a link" is read all in one go and the down arrow jumps straight over the link. With it, "comment" is one line and the link is the next one.

Outlook has two completely different ways of showing an email, and this works in both.

Check boxes: control+F

NVDA has a find box of its own, and it is nicer for reading with than the find bar built into a browser. It starts looking from wherever your cursor is, and when it finds your words it puts your cursor right on them — so the next line down is the line after the words you found. A browser's find bar only scrolls the page and takes your keyboard somewhere else.

Normally you can only reach NVDA's find with NVDA+control+F. These two check boxes put it on plain control+F as well.

  • Control+F opens NVDA's find in a web browser. Ticked to start with. Web browsers only.
  • Bring up NVDA screen reader find when not in Outlook. Not ticked to start with. This one covers everywhere except Outlook. If you tick both, this bigger one is the one that counts.

Outlook is never included. Control+F forwards an email in Outlook, and it always will. Untick both boxes and control+F belongs to the program again, everywhere.

It works on web pages and in edit boxes — any edit box, even a terminal window. NVDA has no find of its own in an edit box, so the add-on lends it one. Password boxes are left alone.

Where there is nothing NVDA can search, control+F is handed back to the program and does whatever it always did.

NVDA+control+F still opens the find box too, with NVDA+F3 for the next match and NVDA+shift+F3 for the one before.

Outlook: the list of emails

Outlook reads each email in the list with the name of every column in front of it, so every one starts like this:

unread, From MacDailyNews, Subject Amy Sedaris joins Ben Stiller, Benicio Del Toro

The word "Subject" is dropped, so now you get:

unread, From MacDailyNews, Amy Sedaris joins Ben Stiller, Benicio Del Toro

You already know the subject is coming, so hearing the word in front of every single email is just noise.

Only the list loses it. The Subject box you type in when you write an email keeps its name, and so does any line of an email that happens to start with the word "subject".

Outlook: writing an email

Pressing tab through a new email goes To, then Cc, then Subject, and then the big box you type the email into. That box has no name, so NVDA used to read out the empty page sitting behind it: "document, page 1, section 1, blank".

Now you hear this and nothing else:

You are now in the message body, type a message.

Tab away and tab back, or leave Outlook and come back, and it tells you again, so you always know where you are.

It only happens in the box you write in. The one in the reading pane, which you cannot type in, is left alone.

Outlook: the spelling checker

Press F7 to check your spelling. When the checker stops on a word you hear:

misspelled, hte, h t e

The word "misspelled" comes at your usual speed, because it is the same word every time and is not the part to listen to. Then the word itself is said, and then spelled out letter by letter, both a fifth slower than usual so you can hear every letter. Your speed goes back to normal straight afterwards, and nothing outside the spelling window is affected.

Moving on to the next mistake tells you that one. Moving around inside the window reads the buttons and the list of suggestions as usual, and does not say the same word over and over.

The window opens a moment before Word has picked the mistake out in your email, so there is sometimes nothing to read at first. The add-on keeps looking for about a second until the word turns up. If it never does, you get NVDA's usual reading of the box instead, because that is better than silence.

A full stop is not a spelling mistake. The checker sometimes stops with nothing but a full stop or a comma picked out, and that used to be read as "Not in Dictionary: period". A word now has to have letters in it, and punctuation stuck to the front or the back of a word is left off.

When the check is done you hear "Spell check is complete. OK button", and nothing else. That is the little window with one OK button in it that comes up at the end, and you hear it whether the checker found mistakes or found none at all — pressing F7 on an email with nothing wrong with it used to say only "OK button", which told you nothing about what had just happened.

The add-on works out that this is the end-of-check window by reading it, rather than by assuming that any OK button in Outlook is this one. The window has to say both that it is about spelling and that something has finished before the add-on will speak for it. Every other OK button in Outlook is left exactly as it was.

You hear it once. That window announces itself more than once when it opens, and the add-on remembers which window it has already spoken for, so you are not told twice. It forgets as soon as you move away, so running a second check straight after the first tells you about that one too.

Checking for new mail

When Outlook fetches your mail it shows a progress bar, and NVDA reads it out a percentage at a time — "ten percent", "fifty percent", on to a hundred. The add-on used to swallow most of that. Every message that lands opens a document, and going quiet after a document opens is the whole point of this add-on, so the download carried on underneath in silence: it would start being read out and then stop part way, with nothing to tell you whether it had finished or given up.

A progress bar is now always read out in full, right through to a hundred percent, however much else is going on. It is the opposite of the noise this add-on exists to remove: something is happening that you cannot see and are waiting on.

How much of it you hear, and whether you get speech or beeps, is NVDA's own setting rather than this add-on's: it is in NVDA's Settings, under Object Presentation, called "Progress bar output". The add-on no longer gets in its way.

Swapping between windows

When you hold alt and press tab, NVDA tells you which window you have landed in. The add-on never cuts that off. It waits until NVDA has said the whole thing before it goes quiet again, however fast you flick through your windows. Press any key and the wait ends, because you have stopped listening and started working.

Landing in Outlook

When you come into Outlook from another program, you get a short description of where you are instead of the window title:

Inbox, list, Josh Kennedy build 1.4, list item, 3 of 57

That is the box you are in, the thing you have landed on, what it says, and where it sits in the list.

What else goes quiet

In Outlook and in Chrome-style browsers (Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera, Vivaldi and others like them), the add-on is a bit stricter. NVDA stops reading out:

  • window titles;
  • the word "dialog" when an Outlook email opens;
  • page titles;
  • pop-up messages from the browser, such as the ones about downloads;
  • the little messages web pages flash up, like "Message sent" or "Your changes were saved".

You can still ask for any of it whenever you like. NVDA+t reads the title, NVDA+tab tells you where you are, and NVDA+b reads out a whole window. Only the things NVDA says without being asked are dropped.

Good to know

  • Press any key and NVDA talks again straight away. The add-on can never leave you stuck in silence.
  • Braille is never touched. Everything still turns up on your braille display, including the things NVDA no longer says out loud.
  • Moving around inside a page always speaks — tab, the arrow keys, and quick keys like h for the next heading. Only arriving somewhere is quiet.
  • Say all — NVDA reading everything from where you are — is never stopped. If you have NVDA set to read a page out loud once it loads, it still will. Turn that off if you want the quiet.
  • The little sound NVDA plays when you enter browse mode still plays, because it is a sound rather than speech. If you turn that off, NVDA says "browse mode" instead, and the add-on does hush that.
  • Two commands, one for each of the settings above, are waiting in Input Gestures under "Mute Browse Mode". They have no keys until you give them some. The add-on takes no keys of its own.
  • A page with a huge number of links is not counted right to the end, so NVDA is never held up by it. The summary then says "over 1500 links".

If something goes wrong

The add-on can keep a diary of what it is up to, which makes a problem much easier to find. It is off unless you ask for it.

  1. Press Windows+r, type %temp% and press enter. That is your temp folder.
  2. Make an empty file in there called muteBrowseModeTrace.on.
  3. Restart NVDA, then do the thing that goes wrong.

The diary is the file muteBrowseModeTrace.log in the same folder. It records every key you press, where you were when you pressed it, and everything NVDA says. Delete the .on file and restart NVDA to switch it off again.

What has changed

3.3

  • "Spell check is complete. OK button" was said twice. That window announces itself more than once when it opens, and the add-on answered every announcement. It now remembers which window it has spoken for and says it once. It forgets as soon as you move away, so a second check straight after the first still tells you about that one.
  • The progress bar for fetching new mail is read out in full again, all the way to a hundred percent. Every message that lands opens a document, and the add-on goes quiet after a document opens, so the download was being read out and then going silent part way through. A progress bar is never the noise this add-on exists to remove — something is happening that you cannot see and are waiting on — so it is now always let through.

3.2

  • Fixed the end of a spelling check saying only "OK button". Pressing F7 on an email with nothing wrong with it went straight to that little window with one OK button in it, and all you heard was the button — no word about the check having run at all. You now hear "Spell check is complete. OK button".
  • The same thing happened at the end of a check that had found mistakes, because that window is a window in its own right rather than part of the spelling window, so nothing connected the two.
  • The add-on now works out that this is the end-of-check window by reading what it says, rather than by assuming any OK button in Outlook is that one. It has to say both that it is about spelling and that something has finished. Every other OK button in Outlook is untouched.

3.1

  • Fixed the spelling checker saying nothing at all. The add-on was looking for the box that shows the mistake by one window class when Word uses either of two, and was reading the mistake out of the box's selection rather than asking the box what the mistake is. So it found nothing, having already silenced NVDA's own reading of the box, and you got silence. It now asks in five different ways, starting with the one NVDA itself uses, and falls through to what Word has highlighted in your email if the box will not answer.
  • The announcement is now "misspelled", then the word said and spelled out a fifth slower than usual, with your speed back to normal straight afterwards. It used to say "Not in Dictionary" and the word at full speed and only slow down for the letters.
  • NVDA is now only silenced where there is something to say instead, so a spelling window the add-on cannot make sense of is read out the way it always was.

3.0.1

  • Fixed the first mistake of a spelling check not being said at all. The window opens before Word has picked the mistake out in your email, so at the moment it appears there is nothing to read yet, and the add-on went quiet instead of looking again. It now keeps looking for about a second.
  • Opening the checker twice on the same word now says it both times.
  • Tabbing around the spelling window reads the buttons and the list of suggestions as usual again.
  • If the word can never be worked out, you get NVDA's usual reading of the box rather than silence.

3.0

  • The spelling checker now says "Not in Dictionary" and the word before spelling it out.
  • When the check is finished you hear "Spell check is complete. OK button" and nothing else. The word "unavailable", and your email being read back to you, are both gone.
  • A full stop is no longer called a spelling mistake.
  • Writing an email in classic Outlook no longer begins with "document, page 1, section 1, blank". You just get "You are now in the message body, type a message."
  • The list of emails no longer says "Subject" in front of every email's subject.
  • All of the documentation was rewritten in plain English.
